## Finding the contact point

Start from the pocket, not the cue ball:

1. **The object-ball path.** Visualise a line from the target pocket
   through the object ball. That's the direction the object ball needs to
   travel.
2. **The true contact point.** Because both balls have physical size, you
   can't just aim the centre of the cue ball at the centre of the object
   ball for an angled shot. The true contact point is determined
   geometrically from the object-ball line to the pocket and the cue
   ball's approach line — it's the point on the object ball's surface the
   cue ball must contact so the object ball travels along that required
   line.
3. **The ghost ball.** Imagine a cue ball touching the object ball at that
   contact point. This imaginary ball is the same size as the real cue
   ball, and its centre gives you the cue ball's aiming reference.
4. **The same routine.** After finding the contact point, the sequence
   doesn't change: stand and establish the aiming line, enter the stance,
   align the cue, lower into position, and use the same stable stroke from
   Week 3.

## Think it through

- Which pocket line is correct for the shot in front of you?
- Where is the contact point?
- Which ghost-ball position is correct?
- Are you aiming too thick, or too thin?
