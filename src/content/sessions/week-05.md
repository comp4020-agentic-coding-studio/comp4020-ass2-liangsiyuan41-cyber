## Contact thickness

Full, thick, half-ball and thin hits change how much of the object ball the
cue ball actually touches. That changes how energy and direction are
shared between the two balls. This isn't advanced collision physics — it's
a beginner reference for "how much ball did I hit."

## Two separate paths

Read the object ball and the cue ball as two separate questions:

- The object ball travels toward the pocket you selected.
- The cue ball leaves the collision along a different path.

Don't expect one line to describe both.

## The tangent line

When the cue ball reaches the object ball in a roughly stun (sliding)
state, it initially separates at approximately 90 degrees to the
object-ball path. Treat 90 degrees as a useful starting reference for this
one situation — a stun-state cue ball — not a rule that applies to every
shot regardless of spin.

## Predict before you hit

The core habit this week introduces isn't a formula — it's a habit:
before playing the shot, say (out loud or to yourself) where you expect
the cue ball to go. Getting the prediction wrong is a normal part of
learning to read contact and spin; the value is in comparing the
prediction to what actually happens.

## A question for next week

What happens if the cue ball arrives with forward spin or backspin
instead? In short: forward spin (follow) tends to pull the final path to
less than 90 degrees, and backspin (draw) tends to push it to more than 90
degrees. With a firm follow or draw, the cue ball can still travel close to
the tangent line for a short distance right after impact, before the
remaining spin bends the path forward or backward. Week 6 picks this up in
full — for now, just notice that the tangent line is a starting point, not
the whole story.
